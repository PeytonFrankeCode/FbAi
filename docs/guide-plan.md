# Guides plan

Twelve guides to write for **Guides & News** (`/news`). They are the part of the site
AdSense is looking for: original writing, from someone who collects, that no other
site has.

## The rules that matter for approval

- **Write them yourself.** The policy that rejected the site is aimed at pages
  produced at scale with nothing original in them. Text mass-produced by an AI tool is
  that, and it reads like that. Your own opinions, your own mistakes and your own
  pulls are what make a page "high value".
- **Use your data.** Every guide below has a live table from the site's own sales
  (the `{{sold-table}}` shortcode). Numbers nobody else has, explained by a person:
  that is the combination to aim for.
- **800–1,500 words each.** Long enough to say something real. Not padded.
- **Your own photos** where you can: a card you own, a slab, a pull. They are original
  content too.
- **Publish over a few weeks**, not all on one day, then reapply. Six good guides
  are enough to start; twelve is better.

## How to publish

1. Open `https://thecardhuddle.com/admin-news.html` and unlock it with your admin key.
2. **+ New article.** Headline, then a one- or two-sentence standfirst (it becomes the
   search snippet), then the body in Markdown: `## Heading`, `**bold**`, `- list`,
   `> quote`, `[text](/sets/2024-panini-prizm-football/)`.
3. Put your own name in **Author**. A real byline counts for a review.
4. **Insert sold-data table** drops in a live table, e.g.
   `{{sold-table player="Jayden Daniels" days=90}}`. It shows the player's raw sales
   by parallel, average price and count, and updates on its own.
5. **Save draft** while writing, **Publish** when done. A future publish date holds it
   back until then.

The section stays out of search while it is empty. It joins the index with the first
published guide, and each guide is added to `sitemap-news.xml` automatically.

## The twelve

### 1. How to read a Prizm rainbow: what each parallel is actually worth
**Category:** Set Guides
- Why does a Silver sell for 3–5× the base, and when does it not?
- Which colors collectors chase versus what is merely numbered lower?
- Your own rainbow: which one did you chase, and was it worth it?
- Data: `{{sold-table player="Jayden Daniels" days=90}}`. Walk down the table and
  explain the jumps.

### 2. Raw or PSA 10? When grading a rookie pays for itself
**Category:** Grading
- What grading costs you (fee, shipping, the wait) against the gap between raw and
  10 prices on the site.
- When does a 9 lose you money?
- Your own submissions: what came back, and what you would do differently.
- Data: one star rookie's table, plus the grade breakdown on his player page.

### 3. Why 1986 Topps Jerry Rice is still one of the most-sold football cards
**Category:** Collecting
- The site's own boards rank it near the top by sales count. Why does a 1986 card
  still move that much?
- Centering, condition and fakes: what to check before buying one.
- Data: `{{sold-table player="Jerry Rice" days=90}}`.

### 4. Numbered cards explained: /99, /25, /10 and 1/1
**Category:** Collecting
- What a print run means, and how to spot a fake serial number.
- Does /25 really sell for 4× a /99? Use the tables to check.
- The best numbered card you have pulled, and what you did with it.

### 5. A month in the market: what moved and why
**Category:** Market Moves (make it a monthly series)
- Open the Market page: the index score, and the players on the move.
- Pick three movers and explain each move. Injury, a breakout game, a hot product?
- What would you buy or sell this month, and why. Say it is opinion, not advice.

### 6. Short prints, case hits and SSPs: what they are and how to spot them
**Category:** Set Guides
- How to tell an SP from a base card in hand and in a listing photo.
- Which current products' case hits matter (Downtown, Kaboom, Color Blast).
- Your own case-hit story.

### 7. Buying on eBay without overpaying: reading sold prices, not asking prices
**Category:** Collecting
- The difference between an asking price and a sold price, with an example from the site.
- Best offers: why the site leaves them out of averages.
- Red flags in a listing: stock photos, "RC" on non-rookies, reprints.

### 8. Basketball rookies to watch, using a month of new data
**Category:** Rookies (once basketball has a few weeks of sales)
- Who sells most, and the priciest sales, from the site's own boards.
- Your read on next year's class.
- Data: `{{sold-table player="Cooper Flagg" days=30}}`.

### 9. Topps Chrome is back in football: how its rainbow compares to Prizm
**Category:** Set Guides
- What Refractor, X-Fractor and the colored parallels are.
- How the Chrome and Prizm rainbows compare on sold prices for the same player.
- Data: two tables, one Prizm player and one Chrome player.

### 10. How to protect, store and ship cards
**Category:** Collecting
- Penny sleeves, top loaders, one-touches, team bags: what you actually use.
- How you ship a card worth $20, $200 and $2,000. Photos of your setup.

### 11. What I learned building The Card Huddle
**Category:** Collecting
- Why you built it, and what you found in the data that surprised you.
- This is the "who is behind the site" article. Reviewers look for one.

### 12. Vintage on a budget: Hall of Famers that still sell under $100
**Category:** Collecting
- Use the sold tables for a few pre-1990 Hall of Famers.
- What condition matters and what does not at that price.

## After publishing

- Link a guide from a related set or player page if you want. Links back and forth
  help both.
- Give Google two or three weeks to recrawl before you reapply.
