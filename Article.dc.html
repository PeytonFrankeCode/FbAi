Jaxson Dart Silver Prizms cooled 12% while base rookies held flat

Two hundred and six paired sales over thirty days show the parallel giving back most of its July run — while the base card barely moved.

Peyton Franke Aug 28, 2026 · 6 min read
Built from sold data
[COVER IMAGE — 1200×630]

The Silver Prizm ran hard through July. Between the 1st and the 28th it put on roughly a fifth of its value, mostly on the back of two strong games and a lot of people deciding at once that the rookie class was thinner than they thought.

August took most of that back. Across the thirty days ending August 21, our index has the card at down 12.1%, on 206 paired sales — meaning 206 times the same card sold twice, which is the only comparison that actually tells you a price moved rather than a different card being sold.

The base rookie, over exactly the same window, moved 0.4%. That is noise. And that gap is the interesting part of this, because it says the money leaving was speculative money on the scarce version, not a broad loss of interest in the player.

The Gold Ice going the other way is the tell. Ten copies exist. When the people buying at that level keep buying while the /199 tier sells off, you are watching two different markets that happen to share a player.

A parallel selling off while its base card sits still is not the player losing value. It is the speculation leaving.

If you are holding the Silver, the thing to watch through September is whether paired volume holds up. Price falling on thin volume is a different signal from price falling on heavy volume, and only one of them means the market has actually repriced the card.
